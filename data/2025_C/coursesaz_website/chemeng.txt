FUNDAMENTALS OF BIOLOGY I.  4.0 Semester Hrs.</strong></p>\n<p class="courseblockdesc">\n</p>\nEquivalent with BIOL110,<br/>(I, II) Fundamentals of Biology with Laboratory I. This course will emphasize the fundamental concepts of biology and use illustrative examples and laboratory investigations that highlight the interface of biology with engineering. The focus will be on (1) the scientific method; (2) structural, molecular, and energetic basis of cellular activities; (3) mechanisms of storage and transfer of genetic information in biological organisms; (4) a laboratory \'toolbox\' that will carry them forward in their laboratory-based courses. This core course in biology will be interdisciplinary in nature and will incorporate the major themes and mission of this school - earth, energy, and the environment. Lecture Hours: 3; Lab Hours: 3; Semester Hours: 4.<br/>\n<p class="notinpdf coursedraw-hidden"><a href="/search/?search=CBEN110">View Course Learning Outcomes</a></p>\n<p class="courseblockextra noindent"><div class="bubble-hidden"><h6 class="toggle">View Course Learning Outcomes</h6><ul><li>Same as BIOL110</li></ul></div></p></div><div class="courseblock">\n<p class="courseblocktitle"><strong>CBEN120.  FUNDAMENTALS OF BIOLOGY II.  4.0 Semester Hrs.</strong></p>\n<p class="courseblockdesc">\n</p>\nEquivalent with CBEN323,<br/>This is the continuation of Fundamentals of Biology I. Emphasis in the second semester is placed on an examination of organisms as the products of evolution and the diversity of life forms. Special attention will be given to how form fits function in animals and plants and the potential for biomimetic applications. Prerequisite: CBEN110. Fundamentals of Biology I or equivalent. 3 hours lecture; 3 hours laboratory; 4 semester hours.<br/>\n<p class="notinpdf coursedraw-hidden"><a href="/search/?search=CBEN120">View Course Learning Outcomes</a></p>\n<p class="courseblockextra noindent"><div class="bubble-hidden"><h6 class="toggle">View Course Learning Outcomes</h6><ul><li>1. Describe and explain the processes and patterns of evolution, including mutation, variation, and natural selection.</li><li>2. Describe and explain the properties common within the three domains of life and the innovations that arose in evolutionary time as organisms diversified and adapted to terrestrial environments.</li><li>3. Use illustrative examples from key animal and plant physiological systems to explain how form fits function in the context of homeostasis and intercellular signaling, development and reproduction, resource acquisition and transport, and to discuss biomimetic and engineering applications of these biological concepts.</li><li>4. Explain and use the key principles of the scientific process to assess and design experiments.</li><li>5. Evaluate the credibility of scientific information from various sources.</li><li>6. Utilize instrumentation and methods for data acquisition and analysis, including tissue preparation for microscopy, dissection and tissue culture.</li></ul></div></p></div><div class="courseblock">\n<p class="courseblocktitle"><strong>CBEN121. INDEPENDENT STUDY.  0.5-6 Semester Hr.</strong></p>\n<p class="courseblockdesc">\n</p>\n<p class="notinpdf coursedraw-hidden"><a href="/search/?search=CBEN299">View Course Learning Outcomes</a></p>\n</div><div class="courseblock">\n<p class="courseblocktitle"><strong>CBEN304.  ANATOMY AND PHYSIOLOGY.  3.0 Semester Hrs.</strong></p>\n<p class="courseblockdesc">\n</p>\nEquivalent with CBEN404,<br/>This course will cover the basics of human anatomy and physiology of the cardiovascular system and blood, the immune system, the respiratory system, the digestive system, the endocrine system, the urinary system and the reproductive system. These are supplemented as needed with animations, pictures and movies of cadaver dissection to provide the student with a practical experience discovering principles and structures associated with the anatomy and physiology. Co-requisite: CBEN404.<br/>\n<p class="notinpdf coursedraw-hidden"><a href="/search/?search=CBEN305">View Course Learning Outcomes</a></p>\n</div><div class="courseblock">\n<p class="courseblocktitle"><strong>CBEN307. Fundamentals of microscopic phenomena and application to macroscopic systems are addressed. Course work also includes computational fluid dynamics. 3 hours lecture; 3 semester hours. UNIT OPERATIONS LABORATORY.  3.0 Semester Hrs.</strong></p>\n<p class="courseblockdesc">\n</p>\nUnit Operations Laboratory. CHEMICAL ENGINEERING HEAT AND MASS TRANSFER.  4.0 Semester Hrs.</strong></p>\n<p class="courseblockdesc">\n</p>\nThis course covers theory and applications of energy transfer: conduction, convection, and radiation and mass transfer: diffusion and convection. Fundamentals of microscopic phenomena and their application to macroscopic systems are addressed. Course work also includes application of relevant numerical methods to solve heat and mass transfer problems.  Prerequisite: MATH225, CBEN307 with a grade of C- or better. Co-requisite: CBEN200.<br/>\n<p class="notinpdf coursedraw-hidden"><a href="/search/?search=CBEN314">View Course Learning Outcomes</a></p>\n<p class="courseblockextra noindent"><div class="bubble-hidden"><h6 class="toggle">View Course Learning Outcomes</h6><ul><li>Define the basic concepts of heat transfer (e.g. heat flow, heat flux, temperature difference).</li><li>Derive microscopic mass and energy balances for chemical engineering systems.</li><li>Apply Fourier\xe2\x80\x99s law for heat conduction to systems with and without heat source terms and for steady-state and transient operation.</li><li>Solve heat conduction problems involving composite media, standard geometries, and various boundary conditions.</li><li>Apply thermal energy balances together with Newton\xe2\x80\x99s Law of Cooling to convective heat transfer</li><li>Select and apply appropriate convective heat transfer correlations to internal and external flows including boiling and condensation</li><li>Size heat exchangers using the LMTD or NTU method and conduct heat transfer performance calculations using energy balances including identifying controlling resistances for heat exchangers</li><li>Use radiative heat transfer coefficients based on Planck\'s and Stefan-Boltzmann\'s laws of radiation for engineering calculations.</li><li>Apply species balances together with Fick\xe2\x80\x99s Law to steady-state and transient diffusion.</li><li>Apply species balances together with relevant rate equations to convective mass transfer.</li><li>Recognize the differences between diffusive and convective mass transfer including diffusion coefficients and mass transfer coefficients. Use correlations to estimate mass transfer coefficients and diffusion coefficients for specified systems and use these to calculate such macroscopic quantities as component fluxes.</li><li>Design continuous mass transfer equipment and analyze its operation.</li></ul></div></p></div><div class="courseblock">\n<p class="courseblocktitle"><strong>CBEN315.  INTRODUCTION TO ELECTROCHEMICAL ENGINEERING.  3.0 Semester Hrs.</strong></p>\n<p class="courseblockdesc">\n</p>\nIntroduction to the field of Electrochemical Engineering including basic electrochemical principles, electrode kinetics, ionic conduction, as applied to common devices such as fuel cells, electrolyzers, redox flow cells and batteries. 3 hours lecture; 3 semester hours. Prerequisite: CBEN210.<br/>\n<p class="notinpdf coursedraw-hidden"><a href="/search/?search=CBEN315">View Course Learning Outcomes</a></p>\n<p class="courseblockextra noindent"><div class="bubble-hidden"><h6 class="toggle">View Course Learning Outcomes</h6><ul><li>Describe the various principles that are important to Electrochemical engineering, including electrode kinetics and electrocatalysis, double layer capacitance, mass transfer, ionic conduction, Pourbaix diagrams and durability issues, and materials and systems limitations.</li><li>Define the specific areas of specialty in Electrochemical engineering and explain their basic principles (Fuel Cells, Electrolyzers, Batteries, Redox Flow Batteries, Super Capacitors).</li></ul></div></p></div><div class="courseblock">\n<p class="courseblocktitle"><strong>CBEN320.  CELL BIOLOGY AND PHYSIOLOGY.  3.0 Semester Hrs.</strong></p>\n<p class="courseblockdesc">\n</p>\nEquivalent with CBEN410,<br/>An introduction to the morphological, biochemical, and biophysical properties of cells and their significance in the life processes. Prerequisite: CBEN110, CHGN221.<br/>\n<p class="notinpdf coursedraw-hidden"><a href="/search/?search=CBEN320">View Course Learning Outcomes</a></p>\n</div><div class="courseblock">\n<p class="courseblocktitle"><strong>CBEN321.  GENETICS.  4.0 Semester Hrs.</strong></p>\n<p class="courseblockdesc">\n</p>\nA study of the mechanisms by which biological information is encoded, stored, and transmitted, including Mendelian genetics, molecular genetics, chromosome structure and rearrangement, cytogenetics, and population genetics. 3 hours lecture, 3 hours laboratory; 4 semester hours. Prerequisite: CBEN110, CHGN 221.<br/>\n<p class="notinpdf coursedraw-hidden"><a href="/search/?search=CBEN321">View Course Learning Outcomes</a></p>\n</div><div class="courseblock">\n<p class="courseblocktitle"><strong>CBEN322.  BIOLOGICAL PSYCHOLOGY.  3.0 Semester Hrs.</strong></p>\n<p class="courseblockdesc">\n</p>\nThis course relates the hard sciences of the brain and neuroscience to the psychology of human behavior. ADVANCED MICROSCOPY FOR RESEARCH.  3.0 Semester Hrs.</strong></p>\n<p class="courseblockdesc">\n</p>\nMicroscopy is a widely used technique in research to study both biological systems and materials from nano- to macro-scales. The widespread use of microscopy is motivated by technologies that have minimized invasiveness of optical imaging, the variety of information that can be obtained, and the spatial and optical resolution afforded by new and emerging imaging technologies. This course will equip students and researchers with the knowledge to develop and apply advanced optical microscopy and imaging in their research. The course will cover the theoretical aspects which underlie optical imaging, including optical theory, fluorescence, and the interaction of light with matter. The course will then discuss the various technologies that are utilized in optical imaging, including microscopy, fluorophores including fluorescent proteins, optical biosensors, lasers, optical detectors, optical sectioning technology, and quantitative analysis of fluorescent images. The principle of operation of these technologies will be covered as well as assessing their strengths and weaknesses. Finally, the course will introduce a number of advanced optical imaging technologies and techniques which are increasingly being applied in biomedical research and will include hands on demonstrations of fluorescence microscopy.<br/>\n<p class="notinpdf coursedraw-hidden"><a href="/search/?search=CBEN670">View Course Learning Outcomes</a></p>\n<p class="courseblockextra noindent"><div class="bubble-hidden"><h6 class="toggle">View Course Learning Outcomes</h6><ul><li>Understand light and how microscopes utilize the properties of light.</li><li>Understand microscope layout and use.</li><li>Understand fluorescence and how fluorophores are used in research.</li><li>Describe different types of advanced microscopy techniques, including common uses and limitations.</li><li>Understand how to present microscopy images and quantitatively analyze the image data.</li><li>Design and conduct experiments in biomedical research using microscopy and interpret the results.</li></ul></div></p></div><div class="courseblock">\n<p class="courseblocktitle"><strong>CBEN690.
