K-12 FIELD EXPERIENCE AND BUILDING STUDENT RELATIONSHIPS.  1.0 Semester Hr.</strong></p>\n<p class="courseblockdesc">\n</p>\nThis course is designed to provide Mines students with opportunities to participate in, analyze, and reflect on issues in a mathematics or computer science K-12 school classroom setting. The overall goal is for Mines students to understand who their students are, build relationships, and begin exploring learner development and learner differences. Specifically, the course will focus on developing Mines students\xe2\x80\x99 ability to identify and practice basic classroom management, differentiate instruction, ask probing questions, mathematics or computer science content preconceptions, language/activities that promote a growth mindset, and professional language. Furthermore, Mines students will begin exploring the factors that shape school norms and culture. In addition to an on-campus seminar, there is a 25-hour field experience requirement in the student\xe2\x80\x99s assigned partner school.<br/>\n<p class="notinpdf coursedraw-hidden"><a href="/search/?search=MAED262">View Course Learning Outcomes</a></p>\n<p class="courseblockextra noindent"><div class="bubble-hidden"><h6 class="toggle">View Course Learning Outcomes</h6><ul><li>\xe2\x80\xa2 identify and provide examples of differentiated instruction.</li><li>\xe2\x80\xa2 identify and provide examples of formative-assessment techniques used to evaluate what students are thinking during classroom activities</li><li>\xe2\x80\xa2 articulate the value of reflecting on their practice.</li><li>\xe2\x80\xa2 explain different levels of questioning and how to ask probing questions as well as provide examples of how to use these types of questioning.</li><li>\xe2\x80\xa2 articulate reasons for, ways to, and examples of how they built relationships with each and every student in their classroom.</li><li>\xe2\x80\xa2 articulate and document the mathematics or computer science content specific preconceptions that they observed students demonstrate during the field placement.</li><li>\xe2\x80\xa2 identify the school policies and practices of their field placement.</li><li>\xe2\x80\xa2 identify factors that shaped the culture and norms of the school they experienced.</li><li>\xe2\x80\xa2 communicate effectively, model appropriate use of language (e.g., use of proper grammar, use of professional language, and use of discipline-specific vocabulary), and identify unprofessional language.</li><li>\xe2\x80\xa2 articulate the critical role of high ethical standards, including a belief in being committed to displaying ethical conduct towards students, performance and the profession, colleagues, and parents and the community.</li><li>\xe2\x80\xa2 recognize that with quality instruction and hard work, all students are capable of learning science and mathematics; use language, activities and feedback that is consistent with a growth mindset.</li></ul></div></p></div><div class="courseblock">\n<p class="courseblocktitle"><strong>MAED405.  MATHEMATICAL PRACTICES AND THE SOCIAL CONTEXT OF MATHEMATICS.  3.0 Semester Hrs.</strong></p>\n<p class="courseblockdesc">\n</p>\nThis course provides teacher candidates an opportunity to develop the skills to promote students mathematical identity and their understanding of mathematical practices and processes - mathematics is a community of inquiry-as articulated in the Colorado Academic Standards and Common Core. These skills will be modeled, practiced and mastered in the context of authentic mathematical practices (eg. the formation of the quadratic equation through maximization of orange production). Teacher candidates will engage as learners, reflect as practitioners, and finally develop their own 3-day mini-unit. To promote candidates awareness of the social context of mathematics, candidates will explore the historical development of content and perspectives from diverse cultures. In addition this course will prepare students to be able to communicate effectively in a variety of mediums (written, oral, and digital) as educators about mathematical processes and practices.<br/>\n<p class="notinpdf coursedraw-hidden"><a href="/search/?search=MAED405">View Course Learning Outcomes</a></p>\n<p class="courseblockextra noindent"><div class="bubble-hidden"><h6 class="toggle">View Course Learning Outcomes</h6><ul><li>nurture development of mathematical processes and practices. demonstrating these as tools use to solve problems and communicate ideas.</li><li>demonstrate that doing mathematics is a sense-making activity that promotes perseverance, problem posing, and problem solving.</li><li>provide examples and connections for students to see that mathematics is a human endeavor that is practice in and out of school, across many facets of life.</li><li>integrate the history of mathematics into content and share contributions from people with different gender and cultural, linguistic, religious, and racial/ethnic backgrounds.</li><li>articulate how mathematics is based on constructed conventions and agreements about the meanings of words and symbols, and these conventions vary; algorithms considered as standard in the United States different from algorithms used in other countries.</li><li>cultivate their students\xe2\x80\x99 mathematical identity by helping students realize the usefulness of mathematics by providing connections to students\xe2\x80\x99 everyday lives and building their students mathematics self-efficacy by encouraging hard work from every student and demonstrating the belief that every student is capable of learning and using mathematics</li><li>identify and implement practices that draw on students\xe2\x80\x99 mathematical, cultural, and linguistic resources/strengths and challenge practices grounded in deficit-based PRE-ALGEBRA AND ALGEBRA TEACHING TECHNIQUES.  3.0 Semester Hrs.</strong></p>\n<p class="courseblockdesc">\n</p>\nIn this course teacher candidates will be exposed to evidence-based instructional practices to support students learning of pre-algebra and algebra and model meaningful learning opportunities, common misconceptions and ways of thinking, and students learning progressions (i.e., content trajectory). The goal of this course is for teacher candidates is to develop an awareness of 1) the common misconceptions and learning progressions associated with pre-algebra and algebra; 2) students learning progressions in pre-algebra and algebra, and 3) evidence-based and meaningful instructional strategies for pre-algebra and algebra. The teacher candidate analyzes conceptual algebra underpinnings, common misconceptions, and students\' ways of thinking to create opportunities to learn.<br/>\n<p class="notinpdf coursedraw-hidden"><a href="/search/?search=MAED425">View Course Learning Outcomes</a></p>\n<p class="courseblockextra noindent"><div class="bubble-hidden"><h6 class="toggle">View Course Learning Outcomes</h6><ul><li>\xe2\x80\xa2 plan at least the first month of instruction for a middle or high school pre-algebra or algebra course using standards-based lessons experienced in this course.</li><li>\xe2\x80\xa2 construct and evaluate mathematical conjectures and argument to validate one\xe2\x80\x99s own mathematical thinking.</li><li>\xe2\x80\xa2 identify and develop lessons that are designed to build students knowledge as defined in the Colorado Academic Standards in mathematics and literacy. CAPSTONE CURRICULUM DESIGN I.  3.0 Semester Hrs.</strong></p>\n<p class="courseblockdesc">\n</p>\nThis course provides Mines students an intensive teaching experience in a K-12 mathematics or computer science classroom. The goal of this course is for the student to develop and demonstrate competencies in the areas of planning, instructional methods, assessments, creating effective learning environments for all learners, classroom management and organization, content knowledge, and professionalism. In addition to a total of 15 hours of seminars (on campus and teacher professional development), there is a 100-hour field experience requirement in the students assigned partner school. During this semester, the student will be responsible for planning and teaching at least five periods of classroom instruction as well as participate in other school related professional roles and will develop a mini-work sample (min-unit of instruction including: description of setting, learning objectives, three class periods or more of standards-based lesson plans, pre/post assessment, and reflection).  Prerequisite: Completed/concurrent 3 credits of SCED 262; completed/concurrent with MAED 405 or MAED 425. The overall goal is for Mines students to understand who their students are, build relationships, and begin exploring learner development and learner differences. Specifically, the course will focus on developing Mines students\xe2\x80\x99 ability to identify and practice basic classroom management, differentiate instruction, ask probing questions, mathematics or computer science content preconceptions, language/activities that promote a growth mindset, and professional language.
